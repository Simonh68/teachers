Should Schools Have Different Maths Groups?

Read the text and answer the questions on the next page.

1Many schools put students into different maths groups. Some groups learn quickly, while
2other groups have more time to practise. Is this a good idea?
 
4Supporters say that groups help teachers plan better lessons. Students who find maths
5difficult can get more help. Students who learn quickly can try harder questions. Everyone
6can learn at a comfortable speed.
 
8However, not everyone agrees. Some students feel bad when they are put in a slower group.
9They may think they are not clever. Also, students in different groups do not always get
10the same opportunities.
 
12At Green Hill School, students tried a new system. They studied together for three lessons
13a week. In one lesson, they worked in small groups and got help with the topics they found
14difficult.
 
16After two months, most students said they liked the new system. They felt that they could
17learn from one another. Their teacher said, "The important thing is not the name of the
18group. It is helping every student make progress."
