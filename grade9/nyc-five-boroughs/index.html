New York City: One City, Five Boroughs

A city of islands, history, neighborhoods, and five different boroughs.

A

In New York City, a visitor can travel between five boroughs without leaving the same city: Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island. But it was not always one city. In the 1600s, Dutch settlers built New Amsterdam on the southern part of Manhattan. The English took control in 1664 and renamed the settlement New York. For many years, “New York City” mainly meant Manhattan, later including land that became part of the Bronx. In 1898, it joined with Brooklyn, western Queens, and Staten Island. That is how the modern five-borough city began.

B

Geography makes New York unusual. Manhattan is a long, narrow island between the Hudson and East Rivers. Brooklyn and Queens stand next to one another on the western end of Long Island. The Bronx lies north of Manhattan and is the only borough mainly on the US mainland. Staten Island sits to the southwest, across New York Harbor. Bridges, tunnels, trains, and ferries connect the boroughs, so millions of people can move between home, school, and work.

C

The boroughs have different characters. Manhattan is famous for its tall buildings, theaters, museums, Central Park, and busy business districts. Visitors often think its bright lights are the whole city, but Manhattan is only one part of New York. Brooklyn has busy shopping streets and quiet residential blocks, old brick houses, art, music, and the beach area of Coney Island. Both boroughs are full of people, but their neighborhoods can feel very different.

D

Queens is known for cultural variety: people from around the world have brought different languages, foods, and traditions to its neighborhoods. Both JFK and LaGuardia airports are in Queens. The Bronx, north of Manhattan, has a strong music and sports history. Hip-hop developed there in the 1970s, and the New York Yankees play baseball in the Bronx. It also has major green spaces, including the Bronx Zoo and the New York Botanical Garden.

E

Staten Island is often quieter and greener than the other boroughs, with many parks and houses. Visitors can take the Staten Island Ferry from Manhattan and enjoy views across the harbor. New York has changed over hundreds of years, and new people have continued to shape it. The five boroughs do not look the same or sound the same. Together, however, they form one city with many different voices.
